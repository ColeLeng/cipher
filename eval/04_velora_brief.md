# Client Brief — Velora

**Engagement:** AEO Consulting Q2 2026
**Prepared by:** Citable
**Date:** April 2026

## Company snapshot

Velora is a direct-to-consumer formalwear brand founded in 2019, headquartered in Istanbul with manufacturing in the same region. They sell custom-sized wedding dresses, prom dresses, bridesmaid dresses, and mother-of-the-bride formalwear. Estimated annual revenue is in the $8M–$12M range. Team is approximately 45 people, with a marketing team of 4 and no in-house SEO or AEO specialist.

Primary markets are the US (about 55% of revenue), Germany (15%), France (8%), India (7%), and the rest distributed across UK, Spain, Australia, and Canada. They run localized sites at velora.com, velora.de, velora.fr, and a India subdirectory at velora.com/in/.

Average order value sits around $185 for prom, $340 for bridesmaid orders (multi-dress), and $580 for wedding dresses. Custom sizing is the main differentiator versus US competitors like Azazie and JJ's House. Lead time for custom orders runs 3 to 6 weeks, which is the most common complaint in negative reviews.

## Why they engaged

Velora's branded search traffic is healthy — they rank #1 to #2 for "velora" and all branded variants, capturing roughly 4,000 monthly clicks from brand-aware buyers. The problem is everything else.

For high-intent non-branded category queries — "long sleeve prom dresses," "custom wedding dress online," "affordable bridesmaid dresses with custom sizing" — they rank between position 18 and 30 in traditional search, and their visibility in LLM answers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) is inconsistent. Across 142 tracked category prompts, Velora is mentioned in only 18.4% and cited with a link in only 9.2%. Competitors like Azazie and JJ's House show up 60%+ of the time on the same prompts.

The CEO's hypothesis: as more buyers shift from Google to LLM-driven product discovery, this gap will compound. They want to fix it before Q4 wedding season planning hits in late summer.

## Specific goals for the engagement

1. Increase Velora's citation rate in LLM answers for the top 30 high-intent category prompts from 9.2% to 25% within 90 days.
2. Improve mention quality — current snippets describe Velora generically ("Turkish brand offering custom dresses"). Goal is to surface specific differentiators (custom sizing, specific product lines like Harper Sky and Aubrey, price positioning) in at least half of mentions.
3. Build a content engine the in-house marketing team can sustain after the engagement ends. They cannot maintain a high-tempo content operation. Realistic cadence is 2 to 3 long-form pieces per week.
4. Avoid cannibalizing existing branded traffic, which is their commercial backbone.

## Known constraints

- The marketing team is small and stretched. Anything we recommend has to be producible by 1.5 FTE worth of writing capacity.
- They have a relationship with one Turkey-based content agency that produces decent product copy but has weak English editorial polish for US-market longform.
- Legal and brand are sensitive about claims comparing Velora directly to named competitors. Indirect positioning is fine. Direct attack content is not.
- They do not want to invest in paid distribution as part of this engagement. Organic only.

## What we know is working

The Aubrey boho lace wedding dress and Harper Sky chiffon prom dress are their two strongest organic earners by both traffic and revenue. Product pages for these convert at roughly 2.4x site average. Blog content around Turkish craftsmanship and the founder story drives long engagement times (400+ seconds) but low direct conversion.

Their /reviews page and review-related queries ("is velora legit," "velora reviews reddit") drive meaningful trust-building traffic. This is a defensible moat in a category where buyer trust is the primary purchase blocker.

## What we suspect is broken

- Their category landing pages (/collections/wedding-dresses, /collections/prom-dresses-2025) get traffic but rank weakly for non-branded queries. Content thinness is the likely cause — these pages are essentially product grids with minimal editorial.
- Their German and French market content is largely translated, not localized. Branded queries work fine in those markets, but category visibility is near zero.
- They have no content addressing the comparison and consideration phase of buyer journeys ("vs Azazie," "alternatives to JJ's House," "best Turkish vs US bridal brands"). This is exactly the kind of content LLMs cite heavily.

## Deliverables we are committing to

- A 90-day content calendar covering the engagement period
- Briefs for each piece of content with target query, angle, structural recommendations, and citation-bait elements
- Monthly visibility report tracking citation rate movement on the top 30 prompts
- One workshop with the in-house team to transfer the playbook

## What we are NOT doing

- Technical SEO audit (separate engagement if needed)
- Paid media strategy
- Direct content production — Velora's team produces, we brief and review
- Site redesign or UX recommendations beyond content placement
