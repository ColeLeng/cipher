# Client Brief — Lumenfield

**Engagement:** AEO Consulting Q2 2026
**Prepared by:** Citable
**Date:** April 2026

## Company snapshot

Lumenfield is a sustainable home fragrance brand founded in 2020 in Portland, Oregon. They make small-batch soy wax candles, ceramic reed diffusers, and a small line of fragrance accessories. Estimated annual revenue is in the $4M–$7M range. The company holds B-Corp certification (achieved 2023) and is a 1% for the Planet member.

The team is 18 people, including a 4-person founding team that still runs the business. Marketing is led by a co-founder with no dedicated SEO or AEO function. They have one part-time content contractor producing roughly 4 pieces per month.

Distribution is split between DTC (62% of revenue, via lumenfield.com), wholesale (28%, primarily independent home goods boutiques), and a growing hospitality channel (10%) that includes placements at 1 Hotels properties and a recent supply agreement with Rejuvenation. They've been featured in Architectural Digest's clean home roundup (2024) and named a Wirecutter pick (2025) for the Cedarwood Bergamot 8oz candle.

Primary market is the United States, with light international presence in Canada and a recent UK launch via a single retailer partnership. Average order value is $58 for direct candle orders, $112 when subscription is attached, and $340+ for wholesale orders.

## Why they engaged

Lumenfield's branded search is healthy in absolute terms — they capture roughly 2,800 monthly clicks from brand-aware buyers — but it's a small fraction of total category opportunity. For high-intent non-branded category queries like "best non-toxic candle brands 2025," "sustainable home fragrance brands," and "candles like Le Labo affordable," they are essentially invisible in both traditional search (positions 12 to 28) and LLM responses.

Across 98 tracked category prompts, Lumenfield appears in only 7.1% and is cited with a link in only 4.1%. Direct category competitors like P.F. Candle Co., Grow Fragrance, and Boy Smells appear at 4 to 8 times that rate.

The leadership hypothesis: hospitality and editorial wins (1 Hotels, Architectural Digest, Wirecutter) have not translated to LLM visibility. They believe the LLM training data and citation patterns lag editorial coverage by 12 to 18 months, and they want to accelerate that translation through directly citable on-domain content.

A secondary motivation is wholesale acquisition. Boutique buyers increasingly use ChatGPT and Perplexity for category research before approaching brands. Being absent from those answers means missing wholesale opportunity that won't show up in any analytics dashboard.

## Specific goals for the engagement

1. Increase Lumenfield's citation rate in LLM answers for the top 25 high-intent category prompts from 4.1% to 18% within 90 days.
2. Convert editorial mentions (1 Hotels, Wirecutter, Architectural Digest, clean beauty awards) into LLM-citable on-domain content. Currently these mentions exist as press logos on the homepage but have no dedicated content surface.
3. Build category authority around three specific positioning territories: clean fragrance (non-toxic, phthalate-free, asthma-safe), refill-first sustainability, and small-batch craft from Portland.
4. Improve mention quality. Current LLM snippets describe Lumenfield generically as a "Portland small-batch maker." Goal is to surface specific differentiators: refill program economics, B-Corp certification, hospitality channel proof, specific scent profiles (Cedarwood Bergamot, Fig Oak, Linen Sage, Sandalwood Vetiver).

## Known constraints

- Content production capacity is the binding constraint. The part-time contractor can produce roughly 4 pieces per month at current quality. They are not willing to add headcount during this engagement.
- Founder voice matters to the brand. Generic content does not pass internal review. Every piece needs a clear point of view and ideally a founder quote or perspective.
- Brand and legal are extremely cautious about product claims. "Non-toxic" is the only health-adjacent term they will use, and it must be paired with specific qualifiers (phthalate-free, paraben-free). Claims about asthma safety, allergy relief, or wellness benefits are off-limits.
- Wholesale buyers are a parallel audience. Content needs to be readable for end consumers but useful enough that a buyer at an independent home goods shop would also find it credible.
- They will not produce content that names competitors directly. Indirect category framing only.

## What we know is working

The Cedarwood Bergamot and Fig Oak candles are their two strongest organic earners. Cedarwood Bergamot specifically benefits from the Wirecutter pick and drives a disproportionate share of first-time customer acquisition. Product pages for both convert at roughly 2.1x site average.

Long-form blog content about their B-Corp journey, sustainable packaging, and Portland maker community drives extended engagement times (340 to 480 seconds) and shows up reliably in LLM citations when sustainability-themed prompts are asked. This is an existing content asset that is partially working and could be extended.

The refill program is a structural differentiator that has not been fully exploited in content. Refill SKUs convert at 3.4x the site average for repeat buyers, and the program itself is a defensible story (most small-batch competitors do not offer refills, larger brands offer worse refill economics).

## What we suspect is broken

- Press and award mentions live as logos on the homepage with no underlying content. The Architectural Digest feature, the Wirecutter pick, and the 1 Hotels placement should each have dedicated explainer pieces that are LLM-citable. Right now they exist only as external links.
- Category landing pages (/collections/all-candles, /collections/diffusers) are pure product grids with no editorial content. They rank weakly and provide nothing for an LLM to cite.
- The clean fragrance positioning is implicit throughout the site but never stated as a thesis. Specifically, there is no flagship piece on "what clean fragrance actually means" that could anchor the brand's authority on the topic.
- The hospitality and wholesale story is invisible to consumers. The 1 Hotels and Rejuvenation partnerships are buried in press logos. These are powerful trust signals that are being wasted.

## Deliverables we are committing to

- A 90-day content calendar covering the engagement period
- Detailed briefs for each piece with target query, angle, structural recommendations, and citation-bait elements
- Monthly visibility report tracking citation rate movement on the top 25 prompts
- One workshop with the in-house team and the contractor covering the playbook
- Templates for the in-house team to convert future press wins into citable on-domain content

## What we are NOT doing

- Technical SEO audit (separate engagement if needed)
- Paid media or influencer strategy
- Direct content production beyond briefs and editorial review
- Wholesale sales enablement materials (separate scope)
- Site redesign or product photography
